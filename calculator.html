Compare with Sherman Kent’s probability scale

In Words of Estimative Probability (1964), Kent proposed numerical meanings for a small set of likelihood expressions. These are reference probabilities with approximate ranges, rather than sampled distributions.

Kent’s proposed scale · probability percentages, not estimate units
ExpressionReference probabilityApproximate range
Certain100%100%
Almost certain93%87–99%
Probable75%63–87%
Chances about even50%40–60%
Probably not30%20–40%
Almost certainly not7%2–12%
Impossible0%0%

Kent’s original ranges are not confidence intervals and do not specify a distribution. In Kent model mode, we assume a triangular shape with these endpoints and the reference probability as its mode. This added shape is our assumption, not a result of Kent’s work. Certain and Impossible use point masses.

Research background and model assumptions

This experiment builds on zonination’s Perceptions of Probability and Numbers, which reports survey responses from Reddit’s r/SampleSize community. Respondents assigned numerical probabilities to phrases such as “Almost Certainly” and “About Even.” The survey was inspired by Sherman Kent’s work on communicating uncertainty; it is not the original CIA study.

The research provides the motivation for using phrase-specific distributions. Mapping those distributions between your optimistic and pessimistic estimates is this project’s modeling choice, rather than a finding of the survey.

Survey mode maps sampled interpretations of probability phrases onto your estimate range. Kent mode uses a constructed triangular distribution, discretized into one-percentage-point bins by integrating its probability mass. Both sources are mapped onto your optimistic and pessimistic estimates. It treats the CSV percentages as discrete weights, normalizes rounding differences, and calculates equal-tail quantiles.

This is an exploratory mapping, not a measured forecast of task completion. The selected coverage describes the model, not a guarantee about your work. Because the samples are discrete, the interval can contain more mass than the selected coverage. Values are shown to two decimal places.
